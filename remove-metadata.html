    About metadata and privacy

    Photos from phones and cameras often carry hidden metadata called EXIF. It can include the exact GPS location where the photo was taken, the device model, the date and time, and the software used. If you share the original file, others can read it.

    This tool lists what each photo contains and lets you pick what to remove. The picture itself is not re-compressed, so quality does not change. A small rotation flag can be kept so portrait photos are not shown sideways.

    Many social networks remove metadata when you upload, but not all of them, and not when you send a file directly. Removing it yourself is the safe option.

    The PDF tab shows the title, author, software and dates stored in a PDF. The Office tab does the same for .docx, .xlsx and .pptx files, including the last person who saved the file and the company name. Comments and tracked changes are reported but not removed.

    Your files are processed in your browser and are never uploaded.
