How to Compress Images Without Visible Loss

Phone photos are often 3 to 8 MB, far more than a website or an email needs. You can usually make them several times smaller and still see no difference.

Steps

  1. Reduce the size first. A photo 4000 pixels wide shown at 1200 pixels is wasted data. Set a maximum width of 1200 to 1600 for the web.
  2. Pick the quality. For JPG and WebP, a quality of 70 to 85 is the usual balance. Below 60 you start to see blocks.
  3. Choose the format. WebP is usually smaller than JPG at the same look. JPG works everywhere. Use PNG for sharp graphics and screenshots.
  4. Compare. The tool shows the size before and after. Zoom in on the result before you replace the original.

Open the image compressor

Good to know

Each time a JPG is saved again, it loses a little quality. Always compress from the original.

Compressing in the browser means your photos are not uploaded.

Smaller images also load faster, which helps websites rank in search.
